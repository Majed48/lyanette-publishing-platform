Bible Reflections

The Woman at the Well

July 27, 2026 · Lyanette Valiente

“Come, see a man who told me everything I ever did. Could this be the Messiah?”

John 4:29

John 4 tells a story that, the more I read it, the more slowly I read it. Jesus, tired from the journey, sits down at a well in Samaria at noon — the hottest hour, the hour no one goes to draw water if they can help it. But a woman arrives alone, at exactly that hour, and that already tells us something: she was avoiding people. The other women of the town drew water early, in groups, talking together. She went alone, at midday, carrying something heavier than her jar.

Jesus asks her for a drink. It's a small gesture, almost ordinary, but it breaks three barriers at once: he is a Jew, she is a Samaritan; he is a man, she is a woman alone; he is a respected rabbi, she is someone whose story the whole town knew and talked about. Any other rabbi of that time would have crossed the street to avoid speaking to her. Jesus sits beside her and asks for water, as if she were simply a person worthy of conversation — because she was.

The conversation continues, and at one point Jesus tells her something no one else had dared to say to her with love: he knows her whole story, the five failed marriages, the relationship she was living in without being married, and still he doesn't turn away. He doesn't condemn her or lecture her. He simply sees her — truly, completely — and keeps offering her living water. That is the heart of the gospel in a single scene: to be fully known and to remain fully loved.

What moves me most about this story is what the woman does next. She doesn't stay in the shame of having been found out. She runs back to the town — the very town she had likely been hiding from — and says, "Come, see a man who told me everything I ever did." Her story, which once isolated her, becomes her testimony. What Jesus touched with grace, she turned into an invitation for others.

If there is a part of your story you still hide, that you avoid naming even in prayer, this scene is for you. Jesus already knows that part. You don't need to clean it up before coming to the well. Go to him at any hour, alone if you must, and let him see you completely — because where others would see reason for shame, he sees someone worthy of a conversation that changes a life.
