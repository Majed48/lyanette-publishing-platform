Resting in the Waiting

July 21, 2026 · Lyanette Valiente

“You will keep in perfect peace those whose minds are steadfast, because they trust in you.”

Isaiah 26:3

For years I believed peace was something that arrived afterward — after the diagnosis improved, after the bank account steadied, after the relationship healed, after the door I had knocked on so many times finally opened. I lived waiting for peace the way you wait for a train that always runs late, checking the clock, growing tired of standing on the platform. And meanwhile the days went by without my truly living them, because all my attention was fixed on what hadn't arrived yet.

What changed the way I wait wasn't circumstances improving any faster. It was an afternoon reading chapter twelve of the devotional you now hold in your hands, when I wrote — almost without planning it — this line: "peace doesn't arrive once everything is resolved; it arrives when you decide to rest in the middle of what you still don't understand." I didn't write it because I had already mastered it. I wrote it because I needed to believe it, and putting it on paper was how I began to practice it.

Isaiah 26:3 doesn't promise that God will resolve your circumstances before giving you peace. It promises "perfect peace" to the one whose mind — whose attention, whose trust — stays fixed on God. It is a peace that coexists with uncertainty, not one that waits for uncertainty to vanish. That is true rest: not the absence of questions, but the presence of Someone larger than the questions.

Resting in the waiting doesn't mean giving up on taking action, or pretending everything is fine when it isn't. It means releasing a control over God's timing that we never actually had, and choosing, day by day, to bring our thoughts back to him when they drift toward anxiety. Some days you'll manage it with ease. Other days you'll have to do it minute by minute, like someone breathing deliberately to steady a racing pulse. Both kinds of days count as faith.

If you're waiting on something today — an answer, a healing, an open door — I invite you to pray this with me: "Lord, I'm not asking you to explain your timing. I'm asking you to keep my heart in perfect peace while I wait, because I have chosen to trust in you." It isn't a prayer that ends the waiting. It's a prayer that makes the waiting livable. And sometimes that is exactly what the heart needs to keep walking.
