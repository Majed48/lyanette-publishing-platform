When You Don't Know What to Pray

July 14, 2026 · Lyanette Valiente

“In the same way, the Spirit helps us in our weakness. We do not know what we ought to pray for, but the Spirit himself intercedes for us through wordless groans.”

Romans 8:26

There are nights when I sit on the edge of the bed, hands folded, mind completely blank. I want to pray. I know I need to pray. But the words won't come. All I have is a knot in my chest and a list of worries too tangled to become sentences. If this has ever happened to you, I want to tell you something that took me years to accept: that, too, is prayer.

For a long time I believed that praying well meant speaking well — finding the right words, in the right order, with faith visible enough to count. I treated my prayers like little speeches that had to sound convincing, as if God needed me to argue my case eloquently before he would take me seriously. But that belief left me exhausted, and worse, it kept me from praying in the exact moments I needed it most — when the pain was too large for words.

That verse from Romans 8 shifted something in me the first time I read it slowly. Paul doesn't say the Spirit helps us find the right words. He says the Spirit intercedes with groans that words cannot express — meaning that in the moments where human language falls short, God himself takes our silence, our tears, our weary sigh, and turns it into prayer. We don't have to translate our pain into an acceptable spiritual dialect. God already understands the language of a broken heart.

This doesn't mean we stop trying to put words to what we feel — there is something healing about naming our burdens, even in short, clumsy sentences. But it means we don't have to wait until we feel eloquent, or even coherent, before we come near to God. You can pray with a single word repeated. You can pray while crying, saying nothing but a name. You can pray simply by sitting in silence, letting your presence before God be the prayer itself.

Next time you don't know what to pray, try this: instead of straining to find the perfect words, tell God exactly that — "I don't know what to say, but here I am." That sentence, plain and honest, is already a complete prayer. And trust that the Spirit takes what you cannot express and presents it to God with an intercession deeper than any speech you could have prepared. You are not praying alone. You never have been.
